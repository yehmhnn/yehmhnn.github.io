## The Definition (What)

**$n$-step Off-Policy Learning** is an extension of [[n-step Bootstrapping]] where the agent learns the value function of a **target policy** $\pi$ while actively executing and collecting environmental data from a different **behavior policy** $b$.

## Why It Is Important (Why)

In real-world applications, we often cannot afford to let our agent run its current target policy directly (e.g., controlling a real autonomous vehicle). [[Off-policy]] learning allows us to:

- Learn from historical data logs generated by human operators or older algorithms.
    
- Let the agent execute a highly exploratory behavior policy $b$ while still optimizing a precise, greedy target policy $\pi$.
    

However, because the agent is not taking actions according to the target policy, the environmental rewards we observe are structurally skewed. We must mathematically adjust our updates, which introduces a major engineering challenge: **the variance catastrophe** .

## The Core Tool: [[Importance-Sampling]] Ratios

To correct the discrepancy between what the behavior policy $b$ did and what the target policy $\pi$ would have done, we scale the target return by the **importance-sampling ratio** $\rho_{t:h}$ :

$$\rho_{t:h} \triangleq \prod_{k=t}^{\min(h, T-1)} \frac{\pi(A_k \mid S_k)}{b(A_k \mid S_k)}$$

Where:

- $T$ is the terminal time step.
    
- $h$ is the last action index included in the product.
    

### Reading and Interpreting the Ratios

The ratio $\rho$ acts as a reweighting lever that translates the experience of policy $b$ into lessons for policy $\pi$:

- **The Omission ($\pi(A_k \mid S_k) = 0$):** If the target policy would _never_ take one of the actions chosen by the behavior policy, the entire product ratio collapses to $0$. The update is completely ignored. This is mathematically correct because a trajectory containing an impossible action under $\pi$ carries zero information about $\pi$.
    
- **The Up-Weighting ($\pi(A_k \mid S_k) \gg b(A_k \mid S_k)$):** If the target policy is highly likely to take an action that the behavior policy rarely selects, the ratio becomes much larger than $1$. This up-weights the transition, magnifying its importance because it is highly characteristic of $\pi$ but rare in our observed data.
    
- **The On-Policy Reduction ($\pi = b$):** If the target and behavior policies are identical, every individual step ratio is exactly $1$, causing the global ratio $\rho$ to equal $1$. The equations gracefully collapse back into their standard on-policy forms.
    

## How It Works (How)

### Phase 1: State-Value Prediction ($n$-step TD)

For state-value prediction, we must correct for _every_ action taken from state $S_t$ onward . The update formula is:

$$V_{t+n}(S_t) \doteq V_{t+n-1}(S_t) + \alpha \rho_{t:t+n-1} \left[ G_{t:t+n} - V_{t+n-1}(S_t) \right]$$

Because we are starting our evaluation from state $S_t$, the first action $A_t$ was chosen by the behavior policy. Thus, our importance-sampling ratio must start at time step $t$, running from $t$ to $t+n-1$.

### Phase 2: Action-Value Prediction ([[n-step SARSA]])

For action-value prediction, the update formula is:

$$
Q_{t+n}(S_t, A_t) \doteq Q_{t+n-1}(S_t, A_t) + \alpha \rho_{t+1:t+n} \left[ G_{t:t+n} - Q_{t+n-1}(S_t, A_t) \right]
$$

> 💡 **The Critical Index Shift ($\rho_{t+1:t+n}$):**
> 
> Notice that the ratio for Sarsa starts at $t+1$ rather than $t$!
> 
> This is because the update is applied directly to $Q(S_t, A_t)$. By updating this specific coordinate, the starting state $S_t$ and the first action $A_t$ are already **fixed/given**. Since the selection of $A_t$ is not a variable we are evaluating (we are looking at the value of _having already taken it_), we do not need to correct for the likelihood of $A_t$.
> 
> The first random action we must correct for is the subsequent action $A_{t+1}$, shifting our importance sampling window to run from $t+1$ to $t+n$.

### Phase 3: [[Control Variates]] (The Safety Net)

As the step size $n$ grows, multiplying multiple ratios together causes target variance to explode exponentially. To prevent updates from collapsing to zero when an impossible target action occurs, we use the recursive **control-variate form** :

$$
G_{t:h} \doteq \rho_t(R_{t+1} + \gamma G_{t+1:h}) + (1-\rho_t)V_{h-1}(S_t)
$$

If any intermediate ratio $\rho_t = 0$, the target gracefully defaults directly to our current value prediction $V_{h-1}(S_t)$ rather than zeroing out the entire history, breaking the compounding variance loop .

## Variable Definitions

- **$\rho_{t:h}$**: The importance-sampling likelihood ratio between policies $\pi$ and $b$ .
    
- **$\pi(a \mid s)$**: The target policy we want to evaluate and optimize.
    
- **$b(a \mid s)$**: The behavior policy generating our physical transitions.
    
- **$G_{t:t+n}$**: The $n$-step target return .
