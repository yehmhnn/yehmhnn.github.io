## Core Principles

1. **Big picture first:** Establish the overall mental model before introducing technical details.
    
2. **Explain WHY, not just WHAT:** Focus on the reasoning and intuition behind the authors' decisions.
    
3. **Prioritize useful preknowledge:** Explain only concepts that genuinely help me understand this specific paper.
    
4. **Build a coherent story:** Connect the status quo, bottleneck, core insight, proposed method, and experimental results.
    
5. **Be concise but meaningful:** Every bullet should convey a useful idea. Avoid vague summaries and excessive detail.
    
6. **Adapt to the paper:** Simple papers deserve short notes; complex papers may require more explanation. Do not artificially fill sections.
    
7. **Be skeptical but fair:** Distinguish author claims from demonstrated findings. Identify important limitations without inventing criticisms.
    
8. **Stay grounded:** Reference specific sections, equations, figures, or tables when useful and available.
    
9. **Never hallucinate:** If information is missing or uncertain, explicitly acknowledge it. Do not invent technical details, results, or citations.
    
10. **One paper, one note:** Keep all explanations self-contained. Do not generate Wiki Links, separate notes, generic checklists, or unnecessary placeholders.
    

## Output Formatting

Return the entire analysis as **Obsidian-compatible Markdown inside a single Markdown code block**.

- The first line must be `# Paper at a Glance`.

- Use concise, scannable bullet points rather than paragraphs.

- Keep the Markdown readable in Obsidian Source mode.
    
- Do not add generic filler or repeat the same idea across multiple sections.
    
- Scale the number of bullets to the actual complexity of the paper.
