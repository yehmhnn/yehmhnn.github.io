# Abstract & Key Takeaways

- **Core Contribution:** Introduces **MATH-SHEPHERD**, a process-oriented Process Reward Model (PRM) that automatically assigns reward scores to individual math reasoning steps _without_ requiring costly human annotation.
    
- **Dual-Use Paradigm:**
    
    - **Verification (Reranking):** Evaluates and reranks multiple candidate outputs generated by LLMs to select the highest-quality path.
        
    - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Acts as a step-level reward function to train LLMs via step-by-step Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
        
- **Impact:** Drastically improves open-source LLM reasoning capabilities (e.g., boosting Mistral-7B from **77.9% $\rightarrow$ 84.1%** on GSM8K and **28.6% $\rightarrow$ 33.0%** on MATH).

# 1. Motivation

- **The Problem:** Even state-of-the-art LLMs struggle with complex, multi-step mathematical reasoning due to error propagation—a single uncorrected mistake early on invalidates the entire solution.
    
- **The Bottleneck:** While Process Reward Models (PRMs) provide better step-by-step guidance than Outcome Reward Models (ORMs), constructing PRM datasets traditionally requires dense, prohibitively expensive human annotations.
    
- **The Solution:** Automate step-level labeling by evaluating the "winnability" of a partial reasoning path via automated rollouts.

# 2. Related Work

4 Core LLM Math Strategies:

| **Strategy**          | **Mechanism**                                                                                                              | **Pros & Cons**                                                                      |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pretraining**       | Training on domain-specific corpora (LaTeX papers from arXiv, math textbooks, code repos with `numpy`/`sympy`).            | Builds foundational math knowledge but is computationally expensive.                 |
| **Fine-Tuning (SFT)** | Training on structured Question $\rightarrow$ Step-by-Step Reasoning $\rightarrow$ Final Answer datasets (e.g., MetaMATH). | Teaches structured problem-solving but relies heavily on static, imitation learning. |
| **Prompting**         | Utilizing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or In-Context Learning without modifying base model weights.                              | Low implementation friction, but lacks architectural validation.                     |
| **Verification**      | An auxiliary model (Verifier/Reward Model) scores candidate outputs to rerank them.                                        | Highly effective; shifts the paradigm from pure generation to systematic selection.  |

**Why Verification?** 
Relying purely on an LLM's top-1 response is unreliable. A robust verifier filters out flawed reasoning paths and provides fine-grained feedback loops for model optimization.

# 3. Method

#### 3.3. Automatic Process Annotation

- **Core Principle:** Inspired by [[Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)]], the quality of an intermediate reasoning step is defined by its **potential to deduce the correct answer** (its "winnability"), rather than its mathematical elegance.

##### Component Breakdown

| **Feature** | **Generator**                       | **Completer**                                                                        |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Input**   | Question only.                      | Question + partial reasoning path $(s_1, \dots, s_i)$.                               |
| **Output**  | A full "draft" solution.            | $N$ different completion "rollouts" to see if the path can reach the correct answer. |
| **Purpose** | To provide raw text paths to grade. | To provide statistical evidence needed to assign a grade.                            |
| **Analogy** | A student taking a practice exam.   | A game simulator checking if a specific move leads to a win.                         |

> **Note:** The Generator and Completer are structurally identical at the start of the automatic annotation phase; they simply begin token generation at different points in the text sequence.

##### Labels and Estimation

The PRM is trained using standard [[Cross-Entropy Loss]] based on two labeling estimation approaches:

- **Hard Estimation:** Binary classification based on whether a rollout reaches the ground-truth final answer (Success = 1, Failure = 0).
    
- **Soft Estimation:** Continuous target values based on the statistical ratio of successful rollouts out of the total $N$ rollouts.
    

#### 3.4. Verification Mechanics

To aggregate candidate solutions and determine the final predicted answer, the system calculates:

$$a_{sc+rm} = \arg \max_a \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{1}(a_i = a) \cdot RM(p, S_i)$$

Where the full solution reward score $RM(p, S_i)$ is determined by the weakest link in the chain:

$$RM(p, S_i) = \min(score_{step1}, score_{step2}, \dots, score_{stepK})$$

##### Mathematical Notation Breakdown

|**Symbol**|**Name**|**Meaning**|
|---|---|---|
|$a_{sc+rm}$|**Final Prediction**|The ultimate answer the system outputs.|
|$\arg \max_a$|**Argument of the Max**|Selects the answer $a$ that yields the highest cumulative score.|
|$\sum_{i=1}^N$|**Summation**|Iterates over all $N$ generated candidate solutions.|
|$\mathbb{1}(a_i = a)$|**Indicator Function**|A binary filter: outputs 1 if the $i$-th solution matches answer $a$, and 0 otherwise. This groups solutions by their final answer.|
|$RM(p, S_i)$|**Reward Score**|The step-level minimum reward grade assigned by the PRM to solution path $S_i$.|

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning & Verification Workflow

```
[Stage 1: Prep Math LLMs] 
   └── Fine-tune base models (LLaMA2, LLemma, Mistral, DeepSeek) on MetaMATH for 3 epochs 
       └── Result: Math-capable Generators and Completers

[Stage 2: Build PRM Training Data]
   └── Generators create 15 diverse solutions per problem (capturing right & wrong logic)
   └── Completer (LLemma-7B) performs N=8 rollouts per step
   └── Step labels assigned via Hard/Soft Estimation

[Stage 3: Train MATH-SHEPHERD]
   └── Feed: Question + Step-wise Solutions + Automatic Step Labels
   └── Train PRM Backbones (LLaMA2-70B for GSM8K, LLemma-34B for MATH, Mistral-7B for RL)

[Stage 4A: Verification Pipeline]             [Stage 4B: Reinforcement Learning Pipeline]
   └── Eval Generator outputs 256 paths          └── Policy Generator creates steps
   └── MATH-SHEPHERD scores min(steps)           └── Mistral-7B-MATH-SHEPHERD gives step rewards
   └── Select highest scored final answer        └── PPO updates Policy Generator weights
```


# Conclusion & Evaluation Findings

- **Strengths:** 
	
	* Completely breaks the human-annotation bottleneck, allowing scalable PRM training data collection.
    
    - Significantly outperforms traditional Outcome Reward Models (ORMs) and standard Self-Consistency voting across benchmarks.
        
    - Demonstrates powerful generalization scaling across various backbone architectures (7B to 70B models).
        
- **Weaknesses:**
    
    - Highly reliant on the initial "Completer" model's capability to construct valid rollouts.
        
    - The pessimistic approach ($\min$ step score aggregation) may penalize creative or alternative reasoning paths that manage to recover from a minor semantic slip-up.
